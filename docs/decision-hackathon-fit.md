# Decision-1 in Challenge 1.1 — demo story and evidence

**What it does:** open a modelled encounter → resolve its person, role and service purpose → deterministic permission cut → baseline source facts → a deterministic shortlist over permitted current free-text notes (half the missed-cohort slots reserved for a content-first exploration order) → one Decision-1 call per note carrying a **fan-out** of questions (`relevance`, `time_sensitivity`, `subject_binding`, `urgency` Score) against one `state` → risk-scaled abstention; the Score distribution is retained in the existing `gate_probabilities` map and orders `Records.priority` and the order selected sources reach the generator (first = most time-critical when scored), without affecting inclusion/omission; then add only `relevant` missed records and emphasize `relevant` selected ones → Foundry receives selected sources in that deterministic order (the order instruction is present in Prompt Agent `luvn-narration:8` and the direct prompt; the post-deploy live-services recheck still found only 5/7 comparable sentence-1 matches, so order-following remains observed behavior, not a guarantee) → per-sentence Decision-1 grounding verification → ready briefing. The source-wide `negation` question was dropped from the fan-out: a note can record both a present finding and an absence, so it cannot safely drive a source-wide prose directive; the prose-inversion risk is owned per-sentence by the grounding verifier's `contradicts` question (ADR-0009). Decision-1 is a vendor-calibrated, abstaining System One classifier in the trust path, not a chatbot over records. Its LUVN thresholds (0.6 addition, 0.7 omission, 0.25 margin) are inherited from the vendor's routing example, not calibrated on Finnish data. No model scans the 50,000-person database or decides which customer/professional may access which record. The 50,000-profile delivery is still pending a verified schema; a streaming CSV/JSONL importer with a per-person index exists (`backend/app/delivery.py`) against an assumed shape, and the demo currently uses synthetic fixtures and simulated role lenses, **not real per-professional authorization**.

Fixture caveat: the hosted product exposes nine bound encounters, including the persona-003 same-purpose nurse/doctor policy control, the third persona-003 lens for the therapist capacity, the Phase 3 neuvola/guardian lens and the vammaispalvelut lens. Its deterministic comparison adds no Decision-1 answer key or F2 expectation. The 11-case F2 evidence remains historical (five then-product encounters plus six evaluator-only controls); the six evaluator-only personas under `data/experimental/decision-eval-personas/` remain outside the product index and image. Whether a situation has baseline-missed candidates depends on its fixture — verify coverage before claiming Decision-1 changed a briefing. The separate v2 fault fixture remains mocked and unadjudicated. The 50,000-profile schema is unavailable. See [as-built](architecture-as-built.md) and the [one-page evidence summary](decision-evidence-summary.md).

The first benefit to demonstrate is **fewer missed relevant facts for the current visit**, not an AI risk score. Show the same nurse/person under the two nurse purposes (mental-health versus functioning) and the social-care encounter; check each fixture for a baseline-missed candidate before asserting a delta. Decision-1's label is selection metadata, never a fact sent to Foundry. Structured assessments and review proposals stay deterministic; the human decides what to do. A missing key, `unclear` answer or timeout leaves the baseline briefing. A baseline source is never removed; an incorrect Decision-1 addition can still bias prose. Never promise that every context gets an addition: current fixture coverage limits what can be shown.

## The one-line contrast, and the numbers that may stand behind it

**Innovatiivisuus, as one sentence:** *every other team's AI summarises; ours can only order, filter, and
write verified prose — it cannot decide.* That sentence is about **this** system's boundary, not a measured
comparison against another system, and the demo shows it rather than asserting it. On the presenter surface
(`#presenter-tools`, *Esittäjän työkalut*) the same encounter is computed three times — deterministic only,
+Decision-1, +narration — and **what does not change is stated beside what does**: the permitted records, the
rule-based decision and the structured assessments. The invariant is a test, not copy:
`test_the_ai_layers_cannot_change_the_deterministic_decision` compares the three responses field by field, so
if a layer ever moves `risk`, `actions` or `assessments`, the gate fails instead of the demo quietly
demonstrating the opposite of its claim.

**Vaikuttavuuden kvantifiointi — three kinds of number exist here, and only two are ours.**

1. **On screen, recomputable from that screen.** *Tekoälyn vaikutus* counts the distinct source labels and
   form types among the records **rendered in that view**, and says so: it is a count of the synthetic view,
   never a time-saving estimate (`CONTEXT.md:49`). The corpus declares 12 source labels; the card reports how
   many of them appear in the briefing it is describing, which is a consolidation count and not minutes.
2. **Measured by us, with the artifact retained.** The delivery-scale dry run: 50,000 synthetic profiles →
   698,142 records, imported and indexed in 15.9 s, one 19-record encounter resolved through that index in
   8 ms, 0 duplicate record IDs (`../.scratch/hackathon-readiness/results/delivery-scale-dry-run-v1.json`).
   The live trial: `/api/encounters` in 31 ms and the first picture 8.0 s warm against 42.7 s cold at
   `minReplicas=0`. The arrival rehearsal added this round: 360 records over 40 people in the delivery's
   declared shape, reconciled `ready` and imported with 0 skipped
   (`../data/experimental/delivery-rehearsal/`, `.scratch/challenge-1-1-alignment/arrival-runbook.md`).
3. **Somebody else's, and named as such.** The figures **2 h 48 min/day** overall, **42 min/day** perceived
   duplicate recording in client and patient systems, and **3 h 16 min/day** for doctors are from *Selvitys
   kirjaamisen kuormittavuudesta ja hyötykäytöstä hyvinvointialueiden kokemana*, STM Reports and Memoranda
   2025:38 (pp. 25, 27, 48). They are **subjective survey estimates, not time tracking**. They are population
   context, and the repository's rule for them stands: **do not multiply them by any count this demo produces,
   and do not state a saving per encounter or per form.** A count of sources on one screen is evidence of
   consolidation; it is not evidence of minutes.

**The beat that answers *"tietoa jää huomaamatta"*.** The one deterministic place two sources are set against
each other is the medication conflict, and it now says which source claims what instead of reporting that two
sources differ: *"<lääke>: lääkelista näyttää lääkityksen aktiivisena, mutta kirjaus kertoo sen
lopettamisesta"*, with both records linked so the reader checks it at its sources, and the next step phrased
as a question rather than a verdict — *tarkista, kumpi lähde on ajantasainen*. The direction is fixed by
construction (`triage._find_conflicts` pairs an **active** medication with a free-text note that says it was
stopped), so the sentence is true of every conflict this code can produce; the risk table's own
`risk-laakitysristiriita` row carries the same finding into the decision. Surfacing a disagreement is not
resolving it: which source is current is the professional's judgement, and no source is marked the winner.

## Evidence added in the final pass

**Forms and duplicate facts.** A record now carries envelope-level form metadata (`form_type`, `version`, `service`) and, where a fact is measurable, a deterministic `concept_key`. The neuvola lens demonstrates the challenge's own problem rather than describing it: one height/weight fact arrives on *neuvolan terveystarkastuksen esitietolomake* (filled by the guardian) and on *neuvolan kasvukäyrä*, sharing `neuvola.kasvu.v1.2026-09-tarkastus`. The briefing renders **both** rows, marked as the same fact, never merged and with no authoritative winner, ordered by recency only (newest first, record ID as tie-break) — ordering for reading, not a truth claim. Near-miss pairs are pinned as *not* matched, because a false "same fact" is worse than no grouping. An approved source-authority table with a precedence rule is the honest follow-on: choosing which of two records is true is a clinical decision this demo has no authority to make.

**Catalogue absence.** Presence is computed from **selected** records only, so an absence statement is about the briefing, never about the person's record or the permitted set. Every reason for absence — `irrelevant`, `budget_exhausted`, `not_shortlisted`, `too_long`, `unavailable`, or exclusion by `policy.cut` — renders as one identical neutral state, *"ei näytetä tässä koosteessa"*. A `due` status appears **only** when a selected record supplies an explicit date, shown with its source; no recurrence interval is ever invented. Catalogue entries are synthetic and illustrative, and RAI may be listed but is never scored.

**Coverage per row.** Selected-but-unassessed notes carry a per-row label, and a note Decision-1 excluded from generation is labelled as such while unassessed notes are labelled as remaining in the model's input. The current cap-80 run on the 42-note fixture attempted 41 notes; all 41 returned usable answers in 409 ms, and the one over-byte-cap note stayed `too_long` and visible. None were not-shortlisted, budget-exhausted, unavailable or invalid. This is one synthetic 41-request concurrent run, not a vendor concurrency guarantee; see `.scratch/hackathon-readiness/results/decision-value-eval-cap80-v1.json`.

**Guardian and life stage.** `consent_holder` (self | guardian) and an illustrative `life_stage` (child | adult) are declared, never derived from a birth year. The neuvola lens is guardian-scoped and does **not** cover `bound-self-012`, a self-consent youth-clinic entry: it is filtered in `policy.cut` before selection, so the child's own confidentiality rights are implemented as a policy fact instead of asserted in prose. The axis is illustrative and non-statutory; represented-person verification, consent expiry/validity and statutory mapping are not implemented.

**The floors are load-bearing (paired ablation).** Both variants replay the *same* recorded answers through the same classifier, so only the three thresholds differ. Zeroing them **adds one record** (3 → 4: `bound-note-009`, in an encounter whose answer key authors nothing) and changes no omission (wrong omissions **0 → 0**; omitted baseline records 7 → 7). The pre-registered expectation **"additions unchanged" was falsified** while "omissions unchanged" held, and the direction is the reassuring one: the floors refuse an addition that a zero floor makes, at no cost in omissions. The five-omission effect recorded under the previous classifier **did not reproduce** here, and the figures above are the Decision-1-era replay; a sensitivity result over one recorded candidate set is not accuracy, and it cannot say how a fresh selector call behaves at zero floors.

**Structured-fact disagreement.** Over **thirteen** authored claim pairs: agreement 2, contradiction 1, absent 4, not-comparable 6 — so **11/13**. Pairing is authored per claim and never inferred from text similarity, and a produced source with no authored pair is reported `not-comparable` rather than dropped, so the denominator covers the whole produced population. This is disagreement between authored artefacts: **not** an error rate for the note, the model or the structured record, and **not** independent validation. The single contradiction — a note denying self-harm thoughts while the paired PHQ-9 item scores 1 — is worth showing as instrument-versus-note conflict.

**Omission denominators.** Unexpected omissions are headlined against *attempted* baseline candidates, with the wider selection-layer universe labelled separately. Gate-refused reads are a subset of assessed, never a separate bucket. The earlier "0/25" without its attempted denominator invited exactly the audit that would have discredited the sound parts.

The ablation and disagreement figures above are claim-checked against their artifacts, and every figure here is rendered by `render_evidence.py` into the [one-page evidence summary](decision-evidence-summary.md).

## What can honestly be said about speed and cost

- The **historical, different-task** Decision-1 six-label batch — recorded in a session note that is no longer in the repository, so these figures have no retained artifact — made 276 calls in 80.699 seconds (about **292 ms/request end-to-end batch average**) and returned 213,103 input tokens / 21,752 output tokens. This is not a latency percentile or a measurement of this purpose-aware runtime.
- the Azure retail price feed lists Microsoft Decision 1 at **$0.042 per million tokens** in swedencentral, the same published rate as the classifier it replaced. At that advertised rate, the historical batch's input tokens would be **about $0.009** for Decision-1 alone. This is illustrative pricing math, **not an invoice**, not the new prompt's cost, and excludes Foundry, hosting, networking, taxes and any plan changes. Do not compare it numerically with Foundry without matched workload and billing evidence.
- The purpose-aware `Picture.selection.decision_elapsed_ms` covers the whole Decision-1 stage. It exposes aggregate reported tokens with `decision_usage_complete`, the shortlist/assessed/not-shortlisted/budget-exhausted/gate-refused coverage counts, and per-note Decision-1 outcomes carrying the raw probabilities only for **baseline-selected and selected relevant** records, so rejected/unclear note identities are not disclosed in the briefing. These counters are incomplete on a failed response, not billed cost. Before making a faster/cheaper product claim, measure a fixed synthetic encounter inventory with Decision-1 on/off, selected-ID quality, first-ready-view latency, API usage and billed amounts, then compare against the previous design on the same workload. Foundry generation plus the Decision-1 verification gate remain separate model calls; Decision-1 does not eliminate them.

## Other Decision-1 opportunities, ranked

1. **Shipped: encounter-purpose note relevance**, directly addressing the challenge's zero-touch prepared information. Typed fan-out answers, a deterministic shortlist, risk-scaled abstention with coverage accounting, and source-preserving additive selection.
2. **Shipped: verify each candidate narration sentence against its named permitted evidence** (`supported/unsupported/unknown`), replacing the generative support judge. A model verdict is still not source membership or guaranteed truth, and the verifier abstains fail-closed.
3. **Later, if there is a reviewer workflow: flag genuinely ambiguous permitted records** for a professional, without removing essential facts. This is a diagnostic, not another blocking call in encounter opening.

Avoid using Decision-1 for identity or consent authorization, selecting the person out of 50,000 profiles, date arithmetic, PHQ-9/IADL calculations, risk/action decisions, or cross-person retrieval. Code and the hosting identity layer must own those boundaries; a Decision-1 judgment is probabilistic and Finnish is not its primary training language. The prior purpose probe stopped at 30 valid responses of 31 attempted, and its labels were not independently adjudicated. The retrospective relevance-only five-addition finding is hypothesis-generating, not a release/clinical safety result. A separate four-case direct chat-completions smoke run against the new Foundry project is recorded in `.foundry/results/`; it does not evaluate the agent version or the shipped Responses API/account. An authenticated hosted run **was** performed: the prior five-product-encounter trial and the signed-in `--0000015` app-path check (including the `bound-note-011` addition and urgency-order Score) exercised the agent version through the shipped project route.
